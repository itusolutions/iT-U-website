TECHNICAL DIAGNOSIS

Computer Hardware Diagnostics & Fault Identification

A computer may appear completely faulty when the actual cause is a single failing component or connection. Proper assessment helps distinguish between hardware-related and software-related symptoms.

RAM

Memory-related errors, instability, and unexpected system crashes.

Storage drives

Hard disk or solid-state drive detection problems and storage faults.

Cooling components

Fan problems, restricted airflow, and overheating symptoms.

Power supply

Power delivery concerns and unexpected shutdowns.

Motherboard

Possible system board faults requiring further technical assessment.

Display components

Screen, cable, graphics output, and monitor-related problems.

Component testing and repair options depend on device design, available equipment, fault symptoms, and availability of replacement parts.

COMPUTER PERFORMANCE

Diagnosing Slow Computers

A computer that takes too long to start, freezes during normal use, or struggles to open applications may have an underlying hardware, storage, or resource problem.

  • Insufficient RAM for current workloads
  • Storage drives experiencing performance or health issues
  • Excessive background processes
  • Limited free storage capacity
  • Thermal throttling from high operating temperatures

STARTUP TROUBLESHOOTING

Computer Not Starting Correctly?

Startup problems prevent access to important files. Symptoms range from a completely unresponsive computer to a device failing to load its operating system.

  • Computer powers on with a blank screen
  • Computer repeatedly restarts during startup
  • Operating system fails to load
  • Boot device cannot be detected
  • System enters automatic recovery screen

STORAGE & DATA AWARENESS

Hard Drive and SSD Problems

Storage devices hold operating system files, applications, and personal information. A failing drive can cause slow performance, file corruption, and potential data loss.

Unusual mechanical sounds

Clicking or whirring noises coming from mechanical hard drives.

Disk read/write errors

Repeated errors when saving, opening, or modifying files.

Disappearing storage

Storage devices failing to appear during system detection or boot.

System freezes

Unexpected system lockups during file transfers or disk operations.

Data Warning: If a drive is physically failing, continued use increases data loss risk. Avoid unnecessary reinstallation attempts. Data recovery is a separate specialist service.

DEVICE CONDITION

Physical Damage Assessment

Physical damage affects reliability and safety. Some problems are visible externally, while others involve internal components.

  • Damaged laptop charging ports
  • Broken laptop hinges or cracked casing
  • Damaged keyboard keys or touchpads
  • Liquid exposure and internal corrosion
  • Damaged power cables and connectors

PREVENTIVE CARE

Computer Maintenance & Care

Routine care reduces avoidable problems. Dust accumulation, restricted ventilation, and poor handling affect equipment over time.

  • Keep ventilation openings clear of dust
  • Use computers on stable, flat surfaces
  • Avoid exposure to liquids and excessive dust
  • Use appropriate power surge protection
  • Monitor unusual fan noises and temperature spikes
